# Statistics probes: the store answers from its own numbers, not from the question's wording

A probe is a third place Jev can look, between the question and the rows (catalogue section P). The store reads statistics it already keeps, returns a short verdict, reads no content, and makes no round trip to the model. Under the bitter-lesson reading of the conjunction result, the case for a probe is cost and attention, not secret knowledge: the model could ask for the same number, but it rarely knows to, and asking costs it a turn.

## P4: is the answer in the store at all? (registered 18 Oct, before the out-of-domain set existed)

OUT_OF_DOMAIN is judged from the question's wording alone, and on real traffic it could not tell an obscure detail inside the store (0.85–0.90) from a question outside it (0.91–0.92). Whether the store holds something is a fact about the store. The probe is the cosine between the question's embedding and its nearest passage: one approximate-nearest-neighbour read, with no rows returned to anyone.

- **In-domain:** the 166 blind sub-questions of the conjunction sets (`../conjunction/questions.jsonl` and `fresh/questions.jsonl`), each about a detail stated in one passage.
- **Out-of-domain:** 80 questions written by a blind agent who was told the store holds only the novel's text. They cover eight groups: adaptations, Stoker's life, real history and places, other vampire fiction, criticism, plausible details the novel never gives, period facts, and characters' lives after the novel.
- **Measures:** separation (AUC) of in-domain from out-of-domain by top-1 cosine, against Jev's OUT_OF_DOMAIN probability from the question alone, and the two combined. Also the share of each group the probe gets right at the threshold that holds 95% of in-domain questions.

**Predictions:**
- **Q1.** The top-1 cosine separates in-domain from out-of-domain with AUC of at least 0.85.
- **Q2.** Combined with Jev's probability, it beats either alone.
- **Q3.** Group 6, plausible details the novel never gives, is the hardest for both: its nearest passage is near, because the subject is in the book even though the fact is not.

## P1: the count probe, end to end (registered 18 Oct, before any agent ran)

There are 16 questions whose exact answers come from the store itself (`truncation/questions.json`, generated by SQL, not written by anyone): 7 counts ("How many passages mention Carfax?") and 9 date lists ("On which dates does Dr. Seward's diary have passages mentioning Renfield?"). Seven are **truncated**: more than 10 rows match, so a top-10 search shows a sliver. The other nine are **controls**: 10 or fewer match, so a search can show them all.

Capable Haiku agents (4 shadow, 4 on) get the plain research prompt: no hint about counting, SQL or limits, and at most 4 queries per question. Answers are structured, a number or a list of dates, and scored mechanically. A count is right within max(1, 10%) of the truth; a date list is right at F1 ≥ 0.8. Cost is read from the query log.

- **T1.** On truncated questions, the on arm is right more often than shadow, by at least 0.25.
- **T2.** On controls, the arms differ by at most 0.10. The probe has nothing to say there, and a capable agent already handles small sets.
- **T3.** On truncated questions, the on arm spends no more queries per right answer than shadow. The argument is cost and attention, so the probe must not buy its accuracy with extra turns.

## Disclosure in the description, no exceptions (registered 18 Oct, before any agent ran)

P1 found that what moved answers was not the count but the fact that `dracula` is the table `sx.passages`, whose columns say who wrote each passage and when. That fact is the same for every query, so under the bitter-lesson reading it belongs in the tool's description, not in an exception. The test uses the same 16 questions and capable Haiku agents, 4 per arm, with exceptions off in both arms. The arms are the **plain** description, and the **described** one (`P8_QUERY_DESCRIBE_SOURCES=on`), which lists each source's table and columns. Each agent works in its own folder, which the P1 contamination made necessary.

- **D1.** On the 9 date-list questions, the described arm is right at least 0.30 more often than plain.
- **D2.** The described arm is right at least as often as P1's on arm overall (38 of 64 then), with no exceptions shown.
- **D3.** The described arm spends no more queries per right answer than plain.

If D1 and D2 hold, schema facts move to the description and leave the exception catalogue. What stays are facts about this result or this question.
